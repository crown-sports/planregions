# Introducing WallGraph and PlanRegions

[中文](promotion.zh-CN.md) · [Interactive example](https://crown-sports.github.io/planregions/) · [Comparison guide](comparison.md)

These introductions describe the tools and their practical value. They are ready to adapt for a project page, a release announcement, or a developer presentation. Nothing here implies a published advertisement, a user endorsement, or an accuracy improvement.

## A 30-second introduction

> Which rooms changed after your model update? A tiny wall gap can merge two regions or connect an interior to the outside. PlanRegions compares aligned instance maps and opens a local visual report of merges, splits, and disappearances. Inspect the affected pixels, then decide what needs review. It also extracts regions with holes and exact pixel areas from binary wall masks. Try the generated browser example, then bring your own labels.

Links: [interactive example](https://crown-sports.github.io/planregions/) · [PlanRegions source](https://github.com/crown-sports/planregions) · [v0.3.0 release](https://github.com/crown-sports/planregions/releases/tag/v0.3.0)

## A short announcement

**One pixel. Two rooms become one.**

In a generated 20 × 30 layout, removing one of 94 wall pixels leaves before/after wall-mask IoU at 98.94%. Removing an inner-wall pixel merges two regions. Removing an outer-wall pixel gives the same IoU while one region disappears through exterior exclusion. This is a geometry demonstration, not a trained-model benchmark.

PlanRegions makes that spatial consequence visible. Compare two integer instance maps on the same original pixel grid, write a self-contained HTML report, and inspect correspondence groups and pixels entering or leaving excluded space. IDs can differ between runs. The CLI can also request review with `--fail-on merge disappeared`.

The [browser example](https://crown-sports.github.io/planregions/) needs no installation or model download. To inspect your own outputs with PlanRegions v0.3.0:

```bash
planregions compare --before runs/before/labels.npz \
  --after runs/after/labels.npz --output runs/review/changes.json \
  --html-output runs/review/changes.html
```

The comparison reports observations. A merge may be intentional, and a disappearance does not by itself prove an error. Use independent truth to assess correctness.

## Why a developer might use these tools

A segmentation model produces pixels. An application needs usable geometry, and a model upgrade needs a way to inspect its consequences. These projects supply that connection with explicit input and output contracts.

[WallGraph v0.1.2](https://github.com/crown-sports/wallgraph/releases/tag/v0.1.2) takes wall predictions into original-coordinate polylines, junctions, and loops. Model adapters and geometry stages can be replaced separately. It helps when integrating an existing wall model into a drawing-review interface or a downstream geometry pipeline. The project documents private-data training, ONNX export, evaluation, and the numerical limits found during reproduction.

[PlanRegions v0.3.0](https://github.com/crown-sports/planregions/releases/tag/v0.3.0) turns aligned binary wall masks into region instances with retained holes, exact labeled-pixel areas, interior points, and explicit merge operations. Its comparison service connects changes between two runs to groups and pixel flow; its local visual report helps a reviewer locate those changes. It works independently of WallGraph, and geometry does not require a neural model.

Useful starting points are a model upgrade review, a threshold experiment, an annotation interface, and a manual correction workflow. Both packages retain the original image pixel grid. Neither infers a physical scale. PlanRegions' geometric proximity edges do not establish doors or traversability, and unknown room types remain unknown without semantic evidence.

## What the engineering contribution is

The work organizes established methods into independently implemented, replaceable stages with testable geometry contracts. WallGraph traces skeleton paths and junctions while preserving original coordinates. PlanRegions retains excluded pixels and polygon holes, records explicit merges, and compares overlap groups without requiring matching IDs or allocating a matrix indexed by the largest ID. The report makes those groups inspectable in a browser.

This is an engineering contribution, not a claim to a new foundational segmentation algorithm. The [method references](methods.md), [reproduction notes](reproduction.md), [validation](validation.md), and [research roadmap](research-roadmap.md) separate implemented capabilities, measured outcomes, and hypotheses awaiting experiments.

## What to say honestly about the limits

Both projects are experimental. No real drawing datasets or pretrained weights are distributed; the public examples are generated by code. Real drawings require a compatible external wall model or your own binary masks. Published evaluation includes negative results: the new full wall-plus-region pipeline regressed, and optional watershed oversegmented predicted walls. The visual report adds inspection capability, not a new recognition-accuracy result.

Comparisons require spatially aligned maps. Every positive overlap contributes to correspondence, so even a small boundary movement can connect a group. Background includes walls, exterior, and filtered space; two label maps alone do not identify why a pixel was excluded. Read the [comparison guide](comparison.md) before interpreting the groups.

## Two reusable project lines

**WallGraph:** Turn wall masks into geometry your application can use: original-coordinate wall lines, junctions, and connection graphs, with replaceable model adapters.

**PlanRegions:** See which regions changed between two runs. Inspect merges, splits, and disappearances in a local visual report, then connect the result to your review workflow.

For issue reports, share a minimal generated case where possible. Keep real drawings, annotations, models, and derived private reports outside the public repository.
